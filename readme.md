# UAV Detection and Tracking Based on Interferometric Radar [with MATLAB Code]

The ability to detect and track unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) is crucial in preventing terrorist attacks and criminal activities. Radar systems are considered the leading solutions in this field, as they can operate over long distances under extreme weather conditions and with proper adjustments. However, the detection and tracking of UAVs remain challenging due to their small size, unusual flight patterns, and their resemblance to birds.
Problem definition: Small UAVs (UAVs) pose a technical challenge due to their unpredictable and inconsistent motion and physical structure, among other characteristics. Small UAVs are generally smaller than other airborne targets and fly at lower altitudes, making their detection more complex.
Moreover, their movements are often unstable, including circular motion and rapid changes in direction, which makes the development of mathematical models for predicting their trajectories and movements complex. Additionally, the radar cross-section (RCS) of small drones is similar to that of birds, which adds an additional challenge for accurate identification and reduction of false alarms.
Traditional methods are often insufficient for detecting and tracking small drones, as they primarily focus on distance and radial velocity, failing to address the complexity of two-dimensional motion. Another key issue is the ability to perform real-time drone tracking while managing radar data that could be significantly affected by noise or measurement inaccuracies. Traditional solutions in this field have not fully leveraged the unique movement characteristics of drones, resulting in suboptimal accuracy and consistency in tracking performance.

Conclusions: The detection and tracking of drones have become a key challenge in modern radar systems, especially with the increasing popularity of small drones in both civilian and military applications. As mentioned in the original paper, the first solution demonstrated the capability of an interferometric radar system to effectively track individual drones. By utilizing measurements of distance, radial velocity, and angular velocity, and employing advanced signal processing techniques such as FFT and Kalman filtering, this method achieved robust tracking of targets with nonlinear and unstable flight patterns.
However, the initial method was limited to scenarios involving a single target, thus not suitable for environments with multiple drones. The second paper addresses this limitation by introducing an improved system capable of tracking multiple UAVs simultaneously. This is achieved through the integration of stretch processing, interferometric measurement, and nearest neighbor data association algorithms. By leveraging radial and angular velocity information, along with the proposed solution that extends the applicability of the improved data association and trajectory prediction methods to more complex scenarios, the interference radar system's suitability is extended.
In summary, the combined contributions of these two papers highlight the development of interference radar technology. The first paper establishes the foundational methods for single-target tracking, while the second paper proposes these methods to handle multi-target environments, providing a comprehensive framework for drone detection and tracking in increasingly challenging operational scenarios. Future work may explore other enhancements, such as machine learning techniques for improving classification or adaptive filtering, to further mitigate noise and interference in real-time tracking systems.
